# ADR Reporting & Documentation

## Patient Case

A 55-year-old patient with hypertension was prescribed **amlodipine 5 mg once daily**. After approximately two weeks of treatment, the patient developed **swelling of both ankles and feet**. The patient had no previous history of significant leg swelling. After consultation with the doctor, amlodipine was discontinued and an alternative antihypertensive medication was prescribed. The ankle swelling gradually improved.

## 6. CAUSALITY ASSESSMENT

**Assessment:** Probable / Likely

### Supporting Evidence

* The reaction occurred after initiation of amlodipine.
* The patient did not have significant leg swelling before treatment.
* Peripheral edema is a recognized adverse effect associated with amlodipine.
* The symptoms improved after withdrawal of the suspected drug.
* The timeline and positive dechallenge support a possible drug-related reaction.

---

## 7. ALTERNATIVE CAUSES

Other possible causes of peripheral edema should be considered, including:

* Cardiac conditions
* Renal conditions
* Hepatic conditions
* Venous disorders
* Other medications

However, based on the information provided, the temporal relationship and improvement after discontinuation support **amlodipine as the suspected drug**.
